Mushroom spawn: a quick guide

Spawn is the living starter that carries a mushroom’s mycelium into a new growing material. This page covers the three main types, how to check that your spawn is healthy, and how to store it.

What spawn means

Spawn is mycelium, the white, thread-like body of the fungus, growing on a carrier such as grain, sawdust or small wooden dowels. You mix it into a fresh substrate so the mycelium can spread and eventually form mushrooms. It works like a starter, not like a seed and not like a spore.

The three main types

TypeCarrierBest for
Grain spawnRye, millet or wheatBags, buckets and straw. Colonizes fast, popular for oyster mushrooms.
Sawdust spawnHardwood sawdustLog inoculation and sawdust blocks. Often used for shiitake.
Plug spawnWooden dowelsOutdoor logs. Simple to use, but slower, often several months before the first flush.

Is your spawn healthy?

Good signs

  • White to off-white growth
  • An earthy, mushroom-like smell
  • Even coverage on the carrier

Warning signs

  • Green, black or pink patches
  • Slimy or wet-looking carrier
  • A sour or rotten smell

If you see colored patches or smell something sour, do not use that spawn. Contact the seller with clear photos.

Storage

Keep spawn sealed, cool and out of direct sun. It is alive, so use it as soon as you can after it arrives. If you need to wait, a cool spot or the refrigerator for a short time is usually fine. Always follow the instructions that came with your product.

Common mistakes

  • Using too little spawn for the amount of substrate
  • Opening and mixing in a dusty room
  • Choosing a spawn type that does not suit your substrate

Questions

What does spawn mean in mushroom growing?

Spawn is living mycelium growing on a carrier such as grain, sawdust or wooden dowels. You add it to a fresh substrate so the mycelium can spread and form mushrooms.

Is mushroom spawn alive?

Yes. Spawn is living mycelium, so it should be stored cool and used while it is fresh.
